<p>Technicolor turned color into a marketable spectacle: a red dress, a blue sky and skin tone could sell a poster. It depended on separation negatives and complex printing; expensive, but it moved color from experiment to mainstream storytelling.</p>

<section><h2 id="section-1">What changed</h2><h2 id="section-2">How to use it now</h2><ul><li>For stylized color, decide what emotion or information color serves; do not push every frame to high saturation.</li><li>When studying early color, note separation processes, stock and exhibition. Do not reduce it to a modern LUT.</li><li>For brand films, borrow the idea of color as a memory hook, but keep skin and product color accurate.</li></ul><h2 id="section-3">Do not misread</h2><p>Do not equate high saturation with Technicolor. Its core was controlled separation and stable projection, not a preset.</p><h2 id="section-4">Technicolor is not a filter; it is three-strip separation</h2>
<p>Three-strip Technicolor recorded red, green and blue on separate black-and-white negatives, then dyed and printed them together. Its saturation is not a post look; it is process plus lighting.</p>
<h3>Common misreading</h3>
<p>“Technicolor means high saturation” is inaccurate. Early two-color leaned red-green; three-color was fuller. Processing and prints of different eras changed the look. Another misreading assumes every classic color film was Technicolor; MGM and others used other processes.</p>
<h3>One use today</h3>
<p>To evoke Technicolor, do not just raise saturation. Control costume and art-department hues first, use hard light for contrast, and keep the grade to a small curve.</p></section><section><h2 id="section-5">On set</h2><p>Early Technicolor split light into three colors at once. Watch red clothing and skin; color was sold as spectacle.</p></section><section><h2 id="section-6">Do this</h2><p>Technicolor was not natural color; it made color a spectacle and sold tickets on “redder red.”</p><ol><li>See how a beam splitter records three colors at once.</li><li>Compare a red garment with skin tone.</li><li>Decide whether color serves spectacle or realism.</li></ol></section><section><h2 id="section-7">Avoid</h2><p>Do not treat Technicolor as faithful reproduction; its red is designed.</p></section><section><p><strong>Use it today</strong></p><p>Put the history back into a job: archive, restoration, teaching or a short film.
