<p>AI video did not start with generation. It moved through filters, style transfer, upscaling, matting and frame interpolation before text-to-video. Each step turned post repair into pre-generation, and pushed authorship, rights and evidence to the front.</p>

## Deep dive
<p><strong>About 8 minutes.</strong> This section does not repeat the method above. It adds three things: two conditions, a counterexample and a reusable model, so you can decide when to change the method.</p>
### Condition A: filters and auto-editing
<p>The prehistory of AI video does not start with generation; it starts with automatic processing: filters, stabilisation, denoise, auto-editing, style transfer. These tools changed what post does before they changed what shooting does. A filter makes colour no longer a fact of the location; auto-editing makes pace no longer a human choice.</p><p>Automatic processing is predictable: give an input and the tool outputs by rule. It does not create; it executes. Understand that to separate today's generation from yesterday's processing: generation changes the material, processing changes how it looks.</p>
### Condition B: generation and synthesis
<p>Generative models turn footage into sampling: you do not shoot a thing, you draw something like it from a probability distribution. This brings two new problems: consistency (can the same character survive across shots) and control (can the director specify action, camera and light). Historically the problem was control; today it is still control.</p><p>The history of AI video is not a line of “more real”; it is a line of finer control: from global filter parameters to local masks to keyframes to separate control of camera, action and light. Realism is a by-product; control is the main line.</p>
### Counterexample: treating AI as entirely new
<p>“AI takes video from zero to one” is common. But auto-grade, auto-edit and style transfer have existed for decades; generation replaces selection with sampling and parameters with prompts. Calling it entirely new misses the earlier control and ethics problems.</p><p>The fix is not denying generation; put it in a lineage: processing, compositing, generation. Each stage brings new control problems and inherits old issues of copyright, truth and labour. New tools do not mean new problems.</p>
### Reusable model
<p><strong>Automation → representation → control.</strong> Automation handles repetition, representation handles likeness, control handles obedience. Three historical stages are still happening at once.</p><p>Judge an AI video tool by three questions: what it automates, what it represents, and how finely it controls. Clear answers give the tool a place; “it looks real” alone is usually not enough.</p>
