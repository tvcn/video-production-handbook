<h2>Sample note: not a real client</h2><p>Composite sample: a 30-second matte lipstick film. Not one real project; product, talent, and shade are composite.</p><p>The point is not to memorize shots but to see what makes each beat work. For a client film, replace every number, brand, and person with your own footage and evidence.</p><h2>Beat by beat: five columns</h2><p>Read time, then beat, then whether picture and sound serve the same job. If one column disagrees, the beat becomes “pretty but useless.”</p><div class="table-scroll"><table class="data-table"><thead><tr><th>Time</th><th>Beat</th><th>Picture</th><th>Sound</th><th>Job</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>00:00–00:06</td><td>Material</td><td>Lip/fabric macro</td><td>Fabric rub</td><td>Texture first</td></tr><tr><td>00:06–00:14</td><td>Movement</td><td>Turn/raise hand</td><td>Ambience</td><td>Body before product</td></tr><tr><td>00:14–00:22</td><td>Product</td><td>Hand opens/applies</td><td>Click/apply</td><td>No pack rotation</td></tr><tr><td>00:22–00:28</td><td>Light</td><td>Same shot soft→hard</td><td>Music shifts</td><td>Texture visible</td></tr><tr><td>00:28–00:34</td><td>Proof</td><td>Lip edge/blend</td><td>Quiet</td><td>No skin smoothing</td></tr><tr><td>00:34–00:40</td><td>End</td><td>Look/leave</td><td>Ambience</td><td>No slogan</td></tr></tbody></table></div><h2>Picture and sound: what to copy, what not</h2><p><strong>Copy</strong>: before/after of one action, hand close-ups, ambience cuts, locked-off repetition. <strong>Do not copy</strong>: slow motion for emotion, music covering location sound, illustrative numbers as results. Borrow shots, not evidence.</p><h2>Three reusable moves</h2><p>1. Shoot the same action twice—failure and success—and cut them together; the viewer compares. 2. Every 15 seconds, give a hand/object close-up to ground abstract information. 3. Do not summarize at the end; leave an action or a question as a door to the next piece.</p><h2>Failure and check: breakdown is not copying</h2><p>The common failure is “the shots look similar, but the proof is fake.” Ask three questions: what happens if this beat is removed? Does the sound stand alone? Is the result checkable? If two answers fail, re-shoot or re-cut.</p><h2>Check</h2><ul><li>Labeled composite, not a real client.</li><li>Picture and sound serve the same job per beat.</li><li>At least one before/after is checkable.</li><li>Music does not cover key location sound.</li><li>Ending leaves an action/question, no summary.</li><li>Structure survives removing any one beat.</li></ul>

## Deep dive
<p><strong>About 7 minutes.</strong> This section does not repeat the method above. It adds three things: two conditions, a counterexample and a reusable model, so you can decide when to change the method.</p>
### Condition A: product evidence
<p>The first job of a product-evidence beauty film is to make people believe what the product did. Every look needs three proofs: the same angle before and after, a close texture shot and the wear state. Do not only show a beautiful face; beauty does not prove the product.</p><p>Control variables in evidence shots: same light, same lens, same background, only product or time changes. More variables and the audience cannot tell product from light. Write an evidence sheet before shooting: which three shots must share conditions.</p>
### Condition B: mood or brand
<p>A mood beauty film does not prove the product; it builds “who you become”. The character's state matters more than the product: where she goes, whom she meets, why she puts on makeup. The product appears in action, not dialogue; lipstick is the last action before leaving, not a product display.</p><p>Mood work also needs a real anchor: a pore, a loose hair, an edge not perfectly blended. Total perfection becomes plastic advertising; one imperfection makes the audience believe a person.</p>
### Counterexample: beautiful, but nobody knows what is sold
<p>A forty-five-second beauty film: model in bathroom, by window, on street, beautiful light, tasteful music. After it, viewers remember the mood, not the product name, texture or who it suits. The brand paid for a mood film.</p><p>The fix is not voice-over; add an evidence block: three seconds of before and after from the same angle, two seconds of texture, one second of wear. The block does not need beauty; it needs clarity. Clarity makes the mood useful.</p>
### Reusable model
<p><strong>Character state → product evidence → texture → afterglow.</strong> Give the person somewhere to go, give the product three proofs, show texture, end on the state after use. With all four, a beauty film is both beautiful and credible.</p><p>Each segment answers one question: character answers “who I want to be”, evidence answers “what the product did”, texture answers “how it feels”, afterglow answers “what happens after”.</p>
